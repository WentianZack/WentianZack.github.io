MYOUNGAE KIM PORTFOLIO

This page records my actions, observations, and personal experience with the website.

OBSERVATIONS

13 QUESTIONS ABOUT THE INTERACTIVE EXPERIENCE

01

What was the first thing you paid attention to when interacting with the experience?

The first thing I noticed was the large project image on the right. Its black background stood out against the white page. I could quickly tell that this was a design portfolio. I also saw the numbered boxes on the left, but I did not know what they were for.

02

Spend two minutes with the experience and create a list of each of your discrete actions.

I first looked at the designer's name and the first project. Then I clicked several numbered boxes on the left. I found small Christmas objects and moved some of them around. I clicked “Merry Christmas!” and the page turned red. I clicked it again, and the page became white. After that, I viewed the project images, scrolled down the portfolio, opened a project page, and moved to the next projects.

03

What part of the experience did you spend the most time engaging with?

I spent most of my time in two areas. First, I stayed with the numbered boxes because I wanted to see what they contained. I also spent time reading the project descriptions and looking at the large images. I moved through the later projects more quickly.

04

What was the most common action in your two-minute interaction with the experience?

Clicking was my most common action. I clicked boxes, changed the page colour, switched images, opened a project, and moved to other projects. I started scrolling more after I entered the detailed project pages because they contained many large images.

05

What is your impression of the intended primary goal of the interactive experience?

I think the main goal is to show MyoungAe Kim's design work in a more personal way. The right side works as a clear portfolio. The hidden objects on the left add some playfulness and show parts of the designer's interests. The website tries to present both the designer's work and personality.

06

How does the interactive experience communicate this primary goal?

The site divides these two purposes into two areas. The right side uses project titles, short descriptions, dates, and large images. The left side uses numbered boxes with hidden objects. This makes the portfolio easy to read while also giving the user something small to explore.

07

What is your impression of how the experience should be interacted with over time? For how long and how many different times?

I think the website is mainly designed for one continuous visit. I could try the boxes for a few minutes and then browse the projects that interested me. I might return if I wanted to check the designer's work, but the interactions did not give me a reason to return often.

08

How does the interactive experience communicate how it should be interacted with over time?

The words “Click the boxes to explore” clearly tell the user what to do on the left. The long project list on the right encourages scrolling. Each project title can also be opened to show more images. There is no fixed order, so I was free to explore the website at my own pace.

09

What other media forms, digital or otherwise, does the experience reference?

The clearest reference is a physical Advent calendar. A traditional one has numbered doors with small gifts or pictures behind them. However, I grew up in China and did not know this Christmas tradition. I only understood the reference after reading the project description. The right side also uses the common format of a digital portfolio.

10

What does this reference/s communicate to you about how you should act when engaging with your research experience?

Although I did not understand the Christmas reference at first, the numbers and the instruction made me click the boxes. The portfolio layout on the right made me scroll, read, and open projects. The two sides asked me to explore the website in different ways.

11

What does this reference/s communicate to you about how you should feel when engaging with your research experience?

The hidden boxes and Christmas objects made the website feel slightly playful. The black lines, white background, and organised layout made it feel calm and professional. However, the Advent calendar did not create a strong Christmas feeling for me because I did not know the tradition. I mainly felt curious at first.

12

What is the most frustrating part of the interaction to you and what makes that part frustrating?

The most frustrating part was moving the small objects. Most of them did not cause any further change, and the site did not clearly explain why I should move them. I could not see how they connected to the designer or the work. Because of this, the interaction felt a little meaningless to me.

13

What is the most satisfying part of the interaction to you and what makes that part satisfying?

The most satisfying moment was clicking “Merry Christmas!” and seeing the whole page turn red. This result was immediate and clear. I also liked how the projects were presented. The titles, descriptions, and images were easy to follow, and the detailed pages were simple to use.
